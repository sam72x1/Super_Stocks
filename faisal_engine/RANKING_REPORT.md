## SECTION H — FINAL VERDICT

**2 — IMPROVEMENT NOT DEMONSTRATED.** The contract's rule for verdict 1 requires all of: budget (✓ — exactly 108 per day), a 98.33 %
cluster-bootstrap lower bound above zero against the operational bot (✗ — C's lower bound is exactly 0.000), a positive point
against the standardized bot (✓ +0.182), leave-one-ticker / leave-one-date minima above zero (✓ +0.143 / +0.125) and both folds
positive (✓ 3 − 1 and 3 − 1). One required criterion fails, so the verdict is 2. The post-hoc D1 could not carry verdict 1 under any
numbers and would also fail the same criterion (lower bound −0.136).

What the evidence does support: under the same 108-name budget, C captured 6 of 22 documented episodes on the decision date
against the bot's 2 and a random list's expected 0.67 — an operational signal worth testing prospectively, **not** a demonstrated
improvement, and **not** a reconstruction of Faisal's behaviour. Early detection is weaker than exact capture (+0.09, interval
spanning zero) and is not established.

## SECTION I — REMAINING GAP

The most important gap is **which names Faisal keeps on his first-stage list and when he moves them** — his own text describes
list → first sort → second sort ("averages, short, etc.") → readiness → pressure, but the evidence labels only tell us that a name
was on a list on a date. Seven of 22 cases fail before ranking because of frozen gate definitions (a model-definition limit that the
contract forbids changing here), and the 15 that reach the pool are split between two attention families that no single daily-candle
key orders together. The gap is therefore primarily an **evidence limitation about list stages and persistence**, secondarily an
**unobservable decision process** (inspection order, intraday pressure), and only thirdly a ranking defect.
